## The problem

Third-party cold storage needed to move to a new facility, and the incoming arrangement surfaced a capacity minimum that was not in the signed terms. Separately, nothing connected fleet size to required pallet capacity, so the storage requirement for planned growth was unknown.

## Why it mattered

Cold storage is a fixed monthly cost with a step function attached. Commit to too little capacity and you stock out; commit to too much and you pay for empty pallets every month. Getting the curve right ahead of a 2.5x fleet expansion was worth real money, and the terms discrepancy needed resolving before it became precedent for the next contract.

## What I built

A pallet capacity model translating fleet size and product velocity into required storage, used to size requirements at both current and target scale, with cost per machine served as the governing metric.

Then I resolved the terms discrepancy and negotiated a revised commitment, using the model as the basis for the scaling conversation. Specific rates, commitments, and counterparties are withheld as non-public.
