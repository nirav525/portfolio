## The problem

Operating automated frozen-food equipment across multiple counties and municipalities means separate health permits, business licenses, and facility documentation in each — different requirements, different renewal cycles, different inspectors. A cold storage facility change triggered re-permitting across the footprint. There was no tracking system and no standing documentation.

## Why it mattered

A lapsed permit shuts down every machine in that jurisdiction. There is no partial credit, and reinstatement is slower than renewal. Compliance is also a gate on expansion — the company cannot enter new geography faster than it can be permitted there.

## What I built

A compliance tracker covering health permits, business licenses, and supporting certifications by jurisdiction, with renewal-date monitoring.

And the operating protocols that county health departments require for a novel equipment category: a transportation plan, an equipment malfunction protocol, and a waste management plan. These did not exist because nobody had permitted this equipment type in these jurisdictions before. Writing them meant working out what the underlying regulation was actually trying to guarantee, then demonstrating that unattended frozen equipment satisfies it by a different mechanism than a staffed kitchen does.

This is a program in flight, not a finished one. One jurisdiction is fully permitted with all inspections passed, one is in process, two are waiting on renewal cycles, and three have not been started.
