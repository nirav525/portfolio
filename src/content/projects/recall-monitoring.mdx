## The problem

Food recalls are announced by the FDA and the USDA as free-text notices. Someone has to read them, work out whether a recalled product or ingredient is in our catalog, and do it fast enough to act. That is easy to do for a handful of SKUs and does not hold up as the catalog grows, or as the person responsible gets busy.

## Why it mattered

For frozen food sold from machines in public places, a missed recall is the worst-case operational failure, and the cost of a slow response grows with every hour the product stays on sale.

## What I built

A scheduled job that reads the live FDA and USDA recall feeds through their APIs, normalizes the text, and matches each notice against every active SKU with fuzzy matching, so a misspelled or reworded product name still lands on the right item. Matches above a threshold post to a Slack channel with the agency, recall class, reason, and the SKUs it touches.

Ingredient-level notices are handled separately from finished products, because a recall of a raw ingredient is an exposure question, not a name match. It sits alongside the lot-code traceability in the [operating platform](/projects/operating-platform), which is what lets a confirmed match be traced to specific machines.
