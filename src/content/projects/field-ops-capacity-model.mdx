## The problem

The company had no instrumentation on its largest controllable cost line. Technician utilization, drive time, mileage, and stops per shift were not tracked, which meant two questions had no answer: is field execution getting better or worse, and how many technicians does the next stage of fleet growth require?

## Why it mattered

Without a capacity model, headcount decisions are guesses. In a startup adding machines monthly, guessing wrong in either direction is expensive — overstaff and you burn runway, understaff and service degrades across the whole fleet at once. It also meant operational improvements were invisible. The dispatch system's gains were unprovable until this existed.

## What I built

A KPI framework and capacity model covering technician utilization, drive time, mileage, stops per shift, and hours per machine, with per-machine normalization so performance is comparable across fleet sizes. The model translates fleet size and machine velocity into required field hours and headcount, which is what turns operational data into a hiring plan rather than a dashboard.

The calculator below is the public version of that function. Note the shape of the curve: headcount rises sublinearly with fleet size, because adding machines within an existing service area shortens the average leg between stops. That relationship is the entire argument for growing density before growing geography.
