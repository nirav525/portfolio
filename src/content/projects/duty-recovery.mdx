## The problem

Six import entries covering overseas machine shipments carried duties assessed under a tariff authority that should not have applied. Recovery was available, but each path had a different cost, timeline, and probability of success, and each entry had a hard protest deadline.

## Why it mattered

The duties were a material unbudgeted cost for a hardware-dependent startup, and recovery paths expire. It was also a decision problem rather than a filing exercise — recovering some entries would have cost more in broker and filer fees than the duty at stake.

## What I built

A per-entry triage framework weighing duty amount against filing cost, expected probability of approval, and remaining time to deadline, resolving each entry to one of three paths: self-file directly, protest through a third-party filer, or decline as uneconomical. Then executed accordingly.

Two entries were self-filed and both were approved. Two went to protest on a multi-month timeline. One was declined because the fees exceeded the recovery.

And one ran past its protest deadline before I had built any deadline tracking, and was unrecoverable. The framework existed after the fact for the remaining five; the first one paid for it. Every import entry now carries a tracked deadline from the date it clears customs.
